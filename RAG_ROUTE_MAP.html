How the order is decided

  1. Prerequisites first. A concept comes only after the ones it depends on. Cosine search needs tokens and vectors, so P comes before C. Full map: 03_RAG_CONCEPT_DEPENDENCY_MAP.md.
  2. Build, then measure, then improve. You build a simple v0, measure it, and only then try techniques. That is why book Ch22 (evaluation) moves up to Phases 1 and 3.
  3. Every technique needs a failure. From Phase 4 on, a technique is studied in full only if your own v0 test results show the problem it fixes.
  4. Your scores steer the route. Part A decides what to teach slowly. A score of 0–1 flags a concept for repair before anything that depends on it.
  5. Reviews return on a schedule: 1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 → 60 days. Score 3–4 moves you up one step, 2 repeats the interval, and 0–1 resets to 1 day.
  6. Gates need evidence. Finishing a notebook is not evidence. Graded answers, code written from a blank file, and logged experiments are.

Approval gates: when lessons can be written

Lessons are written just before you need them, once the gate allows it. Lessons for Phases 4 and later are built around failures from your own v0, so they cannot be written well before that data exists.
